Reading the board

  1. Real rates (DFII10) are the deepest signal. Nominal yields don't tell the full story — subtract inflation expectations and low or negative real returns are what trigger gold's largest rallies, because they crush the opportunity cost of holding a zero-yield asset.
  2. US10Y and DXY are the tradeable, daily-moving proxies for that same real-rate story — falling nominal yields and a softer dollar both point the same direction as falling real rates, which is why they're watched tick by tick.
  3. US02Y is the fast tell on Fed policy, useful for spotting a shift before it shows up in US10Y, but too noisy to call the trend alone.
  4. Central banks set the floor, not the daily move. Sovereign buyers are price-insensitive — they don't sell into rallies or panic-buy dips — so sustained purchasing (like Poland and China's 2026 pace) supports price even through periods when rates or the dollar turn against gold.
  5. Geopolitics can override the rate model entirely. When capital preservation demand spikes, gold and the dollar can rally together — that decoupling is itself a signal that fear, not carry math, is driving flow.
  6. ETF flows confirm whether a move has real money behind it; COT positioning warns when that money has gotten too one-sided. Sustained GLD inflows plus stretched managed-money longs together mean: the trend is real, but a violent pullback is one catalyst away.

PM update: rule 5 just played out live. Hawkish FOMC minutes pushed US10Y up 6bp — normally a bearish tell — but gold rose 0.40% anyway because Iran-related safe-haven demand dominated the session. This is a textbook case of geopolitics overriding the rate model, and it's now the first entry in the track record below that will show whether that override was justified or a head-fake.
